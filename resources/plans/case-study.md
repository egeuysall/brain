Should I write a case study for Ryva?

Yes. But not a polished "case study.\ Write a conversion asset disguised as proof.

Right now, your bottleneck isn’t awareness—it’s the second run and trust. A well-crafted asset fixes both if done right.

### What it should actually do:

1. **Recreate the aha in 30 seconds**\ Skip the storytelling. Show exactly what changed.
   - **Before:** Weekly meeting to reconstruct state.
   - **After first run:** 6 concrete actions surfaced, 2 blockers found.

2. **Show irreversible behavior change**
   This is your strongest signal.\ - They switched tools (doc → GitHub Issues).
   - They run weekly without you.\ - **Hook:** \*"We’d be upset if this disappeared.\* Lead with this.

3. **Include raw output, not summaries**
   People trust artifacts, not claims. Paste:
   - 2–3 real decisions.\ - 1 missed decision.\ - 1 _"do this now"_ action.
     Make it feel like: \*"This came from their repo 5 minutes ago.\*

4. **Emphasize what they didn’t expect**
   That’s where conviction comes from.\ - Example: _"We thought it would summarize. It actually assigned ownership and found issues we missed."_\
5. **Keep it brutally short**
   No fluff. No intro. No \*"About Ryva.\*

### Structure:

- One-line hook (their quote).
- Before.\- What Ryva surfaced (bullet artifacts).
- What changed (behavior).
- One-line outcome.\

### Where to use it:

- **DM follow-ups:** \*"This is what it looked like for a team like yours.\*
- **Reddit replies:** Paste just the artifact section.
- **Landing page:** Replace the generic demo.

### Big mistake to avoid:\Don’t make it sound like a success story. Make it feel like you’re leaking internal team data. That’s what makes people think: \*"Ok, wait… run this on my repo.\*\
